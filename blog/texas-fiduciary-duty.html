Fiduciary Duty Explained for Texas Real Estate in 2026

Published 2026-06-10

Understanding fiduciary duty explained texas real estate is crucial for aspiring real estate professionals. Fiduciary duty refers to the obligation of a real estate agent to act in the best interest of their clients. This concept is fundamental to the real estate industry and is a key topic on the Texas real estate license exam.

Introduction to Fiduciary Duty

Fiduciary duty is a legal concept that requires real estate agents to prioritize their clients' interests above their own. This means that agents must disclose all relevant information, avoid conflicts of interest, and act with fairness and honesty.

In the context of Texas real estate, fiduciary duty is particularly important. Agents must understand their obligations to clients and ensure that they are meeting these obligations in their daily practice.

Agency Relationships

In Texas, real estate agents can represent clients in various capacities, including as agents, brokers, or transaction brokers. Each of these relationships carries its own set of fiduciary duties and obligations.

For example, an agent who represents a buyer has a fiduciary duty to act in the best interest of that buyer, while an agent who represents a seller has a fiduciary duty to act in the best interest of that seller.

Disclosure Requirements

In Texas, real estate agents are required to disclose certain information to clients, including their agency relationship and any potential conflicts of interest. Agents must also disclose any information that could affect the client's decision-making process.

For example, an agent who is representing a seller must disclose any known defects in the property, while an agent who is representing a buyer must disclose any information that could affect the buyer's offer.

Conflicts of Interest

Conflicts of interest can arise in various situations, including when an agent represents multiple clients with competing interests. In these situations, agents must disclose the conflict and obtain the clients' informed consent to continue representation.

In Texas, agents are also prohibited from engaging in certain activities that could create a conflict of interest, such as representing a client in a transaction where the agent has a personal interest.
